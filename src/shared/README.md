# shared

Pure TypeScript with no DOM, no three.js and no imports from other layers: hashing, seeded random numbers, math helpers, URL flags and, later, the command and event types. Anything here must behave identically in every browser and in a Worker.

- `solid.ts`: the `SolidQuery` interface the movement code reads the world through (which cells are solid, and for slabs and wedges the boxes inside them), and a sparse cell store for tests.
- `ground.ts`: the `GroundQuery` interface for smooth terrain (distance, push-out, snap-down), which sits beside `SolidQuery` in `CollisionWorld`.
- `collision.ts`, `movement.ts`, `look.ts`, `fixed-step.ts`: swept box collision (with the step-up a grounded body takes onto a slab or a ramp), the player controller and its tuning, view angles, and the fixed-step clock.
- `block-world.ts`, `blocks.ts`: the `BlockWorld` interface the net layer and the game use to reach the voxel world, and the shape and material tables (names and costs too) with the 16-bit cell state they pack into. A shape id names a kind and its turn: the cube, a slab in the lower or upper half of the cell, a wedge that rises toward one of four directions, right way up or upside down, and a functional module (the Research Terminal, from Phase 5d1, the Basic Turret, the Generator and the Power Node) facing one of four ways, whose material byte is its level. The `MODULES` table says what each module costs and the box of its cell it fills, and `MODULE_FOOT` and `MODULE_SINK` say how it meets the ground. A module's `raises` are the steps of its ladder (the Basic Turret has two, the Auto Turret and the Heavy Turret, each with a name and a price in the material that pays for it), which `moduleLevels`, `moduleLevelName` and `moduleLevelCost` read.
- `power.ts`: power by radius (5g1): `reachDistance` and `inReach` (is a player whose feet are here within so many blocks of the middle of a cell, measured to the middle of their body) and `reachEdge` (how far out along a bearing the reach ends for a player who walks the ground, found by halving the span, so ground that climbs or drops pulls the edge in). The host charges players by `inReach` and the screen draws its ring on the ground at `reachEdge`, so the ring is where Energy stops.
- `shapes.ts`: what each of those shapes is as geometry, built once from the ids: the faces the mesher draws, the boxes movement collides with, and the parts of a cell's sides a shape fills, so a neighbor knows which of its faces are hidden. A module has no faces to draw: its geometry is its body box, which movement stands against and the cursor aims at.
- `raycast.ts`, `build-aim.ts`, `placement.ts`: the grid ray (which block a look ray meets first, where, and the cell a new block would take; a slab or a wedge is met where the shape is, not over its whole cell), the aim rules built on it (what the cursor is on, the piece and the cell a click would fill, and why a click would not work: out of reach, a player in the way, the world's own reasons, or the host's further rules, which the screen hands in as a function: what a piece costs and a heap in the way) and the rules that turn a held kind of piece into a shape id from the face aimed at and the way the player looks (a module faces the player, and where the ground would bury more than `MODULE_SINK` of its cell the aim puts it in the cell above).
- `settings.ts`: player preferences and their limits.
- `quality.ts`, `daycycle.ts`, `camera-pose.ts`: the graphics presets and their numbers, the day-cycle palette, and the pose type the photo mode and the benchmark route share.
- `frame-stats.ts`, `bench-report.ts`, `sweep-report.ts`: frame-time statistics, the benchmark report, and the report for a run over all presets.
- `hash.ts`, `noise.ts`: integer hashing, seeded random numbers and noise.
- `flags.ts`, `view-flags.ts`: URL switches for development and for how the world looks and what the benchmark runs.
- `protocol.ts`, `wire.ts`, `net-config.ts`: the multiplayer message shapes (commands, events, control frames, why a room ended and what to tell the player), the codec that turns them into bytes and back (everything from the other side is untrusted and checked), and every network number in one table.
- `clock-sync.ts`, `interpolation.ts`, `move-guard.ts`: the clock offset between host and guest, how other players are drawn smoothly from late and lost samples, and the host's sanity check of a guest's own movement.
- `room-code.ts`, `names.ts`, `net-flags.ts`, `player-colors.ts`: room codes and what players type, names and tokens, the `?join=`, `?broker=` and `?ice=` switches and invite links, and one colour per seat.
- `block-digest.ts`: a fingerprint of every player-made block, to compare worlds.
- `shot.ts`: what a shot of the pulse blaster is, as pure geometry that the host and every screen share: `traceShot` (the bolt runs straight from the camera and ends at the first block, hill or Core, at the first machine's hitbox before that, or after its range), `rayHitsBox`, `muzzleOf` (where a player's gun is held) and the `SHOT_FLAG` bits and `ShotEvent` the host reports (a turret's shot names the cell it came from).
- `turret.ts`: where a Basic Turret's gun is, as pure geometry that the host, every screen and the model share: `TURRET_GUN` (the pivot of the head above the floor of the cell, and how far out the muzzle is), `turretPivot` and `turretMuzzle` (the muzzle on the line from the pivot to a target), so a bolt leaves the barrel the head is drawn pointing along.
- `vitals-state.ts`: a player's Health and Energy as the screen reads them (`VitalsReading`: whole points, what low Energy does to the legs and hands, whether they stand in the Core's field, and the two countdowns of going down: the seconds until the Core brings them back and the seconds they stay shaken; `isDown` says a player has no Health left) and the read-only `VitalsView` that the host and a guest's mirror both give the screen.
- `survival-state.ts`: what the survival loop looks like from outside: the run's clock (`readClock`: which day or night, the time left, where the sky is), the Outpost Core's health, the crew's respawn charges and whether the run is over and why (`OverrunCause`: the Core fell, or the whole crew is down), the events the host announces (a change of light, a wave coming, a player down or back, the end of the run) and the read-only `SurvivalView` that the host and the guests' mirror both give the screen.
- `economy.ts`: what the economy looks like from outside: the materials the pool keeps in tier order (`POOL_MATERIALS`), the pool as a screen reads it (`EconomyReading`), a heap of debris (`DebrisNode`), a strip (`HarvestEvent`) and the heaps as a screen sees them (`DebrisView`: the list, the strips each has left, a version that changes when they do, and a call when a heap is stripped), joined in `EconomyView`.
