# world/render

The thin three.js adapters for the world: they turn data from `../terrain` and `../scatter` into meshes and materials. This is the only part of `world` that may import three.js.

- `terrain-render.ts` builds the chunk meshes, the triplanar ground material and the ground cover maps.
- `water-render.ts` draws the lake: depth fade, ripples, glitter and the planar reflection.
- `vegetation-render.ts` draws instanced trees (three levels of detail, backlit leaves, wind) and grass and flowers on a separate layer.
- `voxel-render.ts` draws the blocks: one mesh per chunk that holds a block, re-meshed nearest the camera first within a time budget. `structure-material.ts` is the one shader they all share; a block's material id picks its tile from the baked arrays. It takes the terrain's light but tunes it for structures: a cooler, stronger fill in shade, a less orange key on plain surfaces and a little more sky in polished steel (`docs/art-direction.md`, lighting pass).
- `core-render.ts` draws the Outpost Core: a steel plinth with amber trim, four pylons and a frame, and a cyan crystal and ring that turn and bob inside. The crystal is the Core's health in the world (cyan, amber, red; a faster beat and a flicker as it weakens, a flash on a hit, dark and tilted when it has fallen). It reads the health through `follow(source)`, is posed from the stage's animation clock, and is six meshes (11 draws across the passes when it is in view).
- `module-models.ts` and `module-render.ts` draw the functional modules, which are cells of the block world that the mesher leaves out. A model is a few merged parts, one for each look (steel, trim, amber, cyan lights, screen glass, beacon), built in code once and shared by every module of its kind and level, painted in the dress of the material that pays for the level (`modulePalette`: grey-green and cyan for Reinforced Composite, brushed steel and amber for Advanced Alloy); the Research Terminal is 1,544 triangles with a foundation that reaches below its cell's floor so it stands on a slope. `ModuleRenderer` follows the voxel world's list of modules, puts a group of meshes on each one's cell turned to its facing, and moves the screens, strips and beacon from the stage's animation clock. A module without power keeps its shape and plates but burns its lights low: `setPowered(x, y, z, powered)` swaps its glowing looks for dim copies (`DIM_GLOW`, 0.08 of the glow, held still), made the first time one is needed and shared by the modules of a paint; `isPowered` says which it is. The renderer does not know who has power: `src/app/power-watcher.ts` says. The Power Node (5g2) is a relay pylon, a column of three disks with a crystal and a blinking needle on a pad, with a console on its front. The Storage (5h1) is a steel container on a pad, with ribbed sides, a roof with a vent hood and a double door under a status strip, and it never dims. The Bed (5h2) is a steel cot low enough to step onto, with a mattress, a pillow, an amber blanket between a headboard and a footboard and a cyan strip round its frame, and it never dims either. The Light (5h3a) is a floodlight on a mast on a small pad, its head leaning toward the ground with a warm lens under a visor: the one model with a look of its own (`lamp`, a steady glow that neither beats nor blinks), and it dims without power like the turret and the Node.
- `reach-ring.ts` draws where a module's power reaches (5g1): a ribbon of light laid on the ground round a cell at the edge of its reach (`reachEdge`, so on a hill it is pulled in), with a curtain of light rising from it that fades out above the grass, and dashes that march round it from the stage's animation clock. `show(cell, radius)` lays it (again only when the cell or the radius changes) and `show(null)` takes it away; Build Mode shows it round the Generator or Power Node in hand where it would stand, or round the one the cursor is on (the Node's is 8 blocks, the Generator's 6). Two meshes and two draws.
- `ghost-render.ts` draws what the player aims with: a translucent ghost of the block a click would place (the model itself, for a module), tinted by whether it would work, and an outline on the block a click would break.
- `foliage-geometry.ts` and `foliage-textures.ts` generate the tree models and their leaf and bark textures in code.
- `backdrop-render.ts` draws the distant forested hills and mountains that close the horizon.
