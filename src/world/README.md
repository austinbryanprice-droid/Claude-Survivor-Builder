# world

Voxel data, seeded terrain, chunks, meshing and materials. No three.js or DOM here, so meshing can run in a Worker; the thin render adapters go in `render/`.

- `terrain/` is the valley as data: the seeded field, the baked grid, surface-nets meshing and terrain collision. `terrain.ts` bakes all of it from a seed. `viewpoints.ts` holds the named camera shots and the benchmark's flight route.
- `voxels/` is the player-built part: `world.ts` holds the chunked store (`VoxelWorld`, built by `createWorld(seed)`), the placement rules and the dirty-chunk list; `chunk.ts` has the 16 by 16 by 16 chunk and its keys. `VoxelWorld` implements `BlockWorld` (`src/shared/block-world.ts`), which is all the net layer sees. It also keeps the functional modules that stand (`forEachModule`, `onModuleChange`) for the renderer, and its placement rule asks a module to stand on the ground or on a block (`MODULE_FOOT`). `mesher.ts` turns one chunk into beveled geometry as plain arrays (cubes by their own path, slabs and wedges from the per-shape skin that `shape-template.ts` builds once), and, given the terrain's height, shades the foot of every wall by the ground it stands on; `remesh-queue.ts` orders the dirty chunks nearest first, and `demo.ts` builds the `?demo=` structures.
- `outpost.ts` is the Outpost Core's place in the valley: a box of 3 by 3 cells and 4 high at the middle of the crash site, its base at the middle height of the ground under it. `VoxelWorld` holds it (`voxels.outpost`) and answers for it: solid to movement, `reserved` to a block, something a block can touch. `withOutpost` lets the ground query the cursor and the camera use see it too. It is not an edit: it is never saved or sent, and every world works it out from the same seed.
- `scatter.ts` places trees and grass from the seed, and says which of them a block has buried.
- `render/` adapts all of it to three.js. `valley-view.ts` puts the terrain, backdrop, lake, trees, grass, blocks and modules into a stage in one call. The modules are drawn from code: `module-models.ts` builds the model of each (the Research Terminal, the Generator, the Power Node and the turret at each of its three levels, whose head is a model of its own that turns about a pivot), and `module-render.ts` draws the ones that stand, one group of meshes each, and points a turret's head at the end of its last shot (`aim`).

Terrain is static in version 1, so every cell in the voxel world is a player edit, and a late-join snapshot is the seed plus `forEachEdit`. Movement reads two queries: the terrain's `GroundQuery` and the voxel world's cell `SolidQuery`.
