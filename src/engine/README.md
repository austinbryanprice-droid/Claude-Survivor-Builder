# engine

The renderer, camera, input and game loop. This layer may use three.js and the DOM, and it must not import the game layers (`world`, `systems`, `net`, `ui`). See [architecture rules](../../docs/architecture.md). The layers above it hand in what it draws and collides with: `src/app` builds a `GameWorld` and passes it to the game.

- `stage.ts`: the renderer, scene, camera and render loop, with the atmosphere, sun, materials, quality presets and the adaptive resolution governor. A stage knows nothing about gameplay; `setMoment` holds a time of day, `setSkyDriver` lets the game's own clock say where the sky is whenever none is held, and `onBeforeRender` lets other layers update before each frame; `precompile` compiles an object's shader programs ahead of its first draw.
- `game.ts`: the fixed-step loop on top of a stage: input, the local player, the camera rig, the avatar, timing, and the benchmark sweep. It reads the world through the `GameWorld` interface (collision, spawn, benchmark route, build timings), so the valley, a test deck or an empty plane all fit. `teleport`, `loadWorld`, `onTick` and `onBeforeRender` are the hooks the net layer and the builders use.
- `input.ts`: keyboard and pointer lock, by physical key, with the F5 rule. While the mouse is taken it also reports the two mouse buttons, the number keys and the wheel (`onPointer`, `onHotbar`) B (`toggleBuild`), Q (`cycleShape`) and R (`turnPiece`) to whoever builds.
- `camera-rig.ts`: first and third person, boom clearance against the terrain and the cells, sprint field-of-view kick, landing dip.
- `bench.ts`, `bench-sweep.ts`: the `?bench=1` state machine (warm-up, a route counted in simulation ticks, a report) and the sweep that runs it once for each quality preset, with the governor off.
- `atmosphere.ts`, `lighting.ts`, `sky.ts`, `materials.ts`, `lit-material.ts`, `texture-bake.ts`, `pipeline.ts`, `perf.ts`, `glsl/`, `post/`: sky, haze, sun and shadows, the shared lit shaders, the procedural textures (noise and ground at start-up, the tiles block faces show the first time a block is drawn), the post-processing chain, and the frame governor and GPU timer.
- `avatar.ts`: the third-person body; it takes an accent colour, so the other players in a room wear their seat's.
- `remote-players.ts`: the other players in a room: a suit per seat in the seat's colour, walking and turning as its owner does, with a name tag. Poses come already interpolated from the network layer.
- `enemy-render.ts`: the machines that come for the Core, as one instanced mesh built from boxes in code: a six-legged crawler of dark steel with amber joints and a red eye, in the world's own sun, shadows and haze. The legs, the jaws, the lunge of a bite, a hit's flash and the rise out of the ground are done in the vertex shader from a few numbers per machine, so a hundred cost one draw call and one more into the shadow map. Poses come already interpolated from the network layer.
- `shot-fx.ts`: the light of the pulse blaster: the flash at the muzzle, the bolt streaking to its mark, the sparks where it lands and the burst of a machine felled, as two instanced meshes (bolts and sparks) drawn additively into the HDR target, so a firefight is two draw calls. What an effect does while it plays is worked out in the vertex shader from the moment it was born, so the CPU writes a few numbers when something happens and nothing while it plays out; strikes and bursts take a delay so they wait for the bolt to land. A strip of a heap of debris (`strip`) is a flash and a spray of amber scrap in the same two meshes.
- `debris-render.ts`: the heaps of wreckage the crew strips, as one instanced mesh built from boxes, cylinders and bent slabs in code (a buckled hull plate, a rib of the station's frame, crates, barrels or a broken console on top, scorched rubble and a mast with an amber lamp), in the world's own sun, shadows and haze. A heap is dressed by its grade (5c): Scrap is the rust-and-paint patchwork, Reinforced Composite grey-green layered panels with a cyan strip and a cyan lamp, Advanced Alloy brushed dark steel with an amber inlay and a white lamp, all from one model and one draw. The model is made in layers and the vertex shader takes them away as strips are taken, the top of the pile first; it also does the rise out of the ground at a dawn and the sink when a heap is stripped bare. The renderer keeps only what the picture needs and the host does not say (the eased share left, the flash of a strip, the glow of the cursor) and draws what the network layer's `DebrisView` holds.
