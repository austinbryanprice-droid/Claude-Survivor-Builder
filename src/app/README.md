# app

The composition root: the one place that knows the engine, the world and the interface together. `main.ts` calls `createApp`, which builds the stage, the game loop and the HUD and puts the valley into them. Nothing here is game logic; it is wiring.

- `create-app.ts` reads the URL switches and the saved settings, builds the `Stage`, creates the `Game` with a `createWorld` callback, adds the HUD and starts the loop. With `?debug` it exposes `window.__ashbound` (`{ stage, game, build }`, and `mp` once multiplayer has loaded, `intro` and `title` while they exist, `audio`: the game's audio hub, `render`, which draws the film's soundtrack to samples, and `renderValley`, which draws a night of the valley's sound, and `sound`: what the valley's sound has started and heard). Unless `?bench=1` is on, it then loads `attach-multiplayer.ts` on demand, so the benchmark page and the first paint never pay for the networking code.
- `valley-world.ts` builds a `ValleyWorld` for a seed: the baked terrain, the `VoxelWorld` on top of it (the `BlockWorld` the net layer builds on; empty, unless `?demo=` raises a structure), the `ValleyView` that draws the terrain, lake, trees, grass and blocks, and the collision, spawn and benchmark route the game reads through the engine's `GameWorld` interface. `Game.loadWorld(seed)` calls the same callback again for a new seed. It also makes the world's `PowerWatcher` (`power-watcher.ts`, 5g2).
- `power-watcher.ts` is which of the crew's modules have power, as this screen sees it, and the lights to match: it runs the host's own `PowerGrid` over the screen's copy of the world's modules each time one is placed, broken or raised, and tells the module renderer which turrets, Nodes and Lights have none, so their lights go down (`world/render` cannot ask the simulation itself). The host reads the same function for the pace of its turrets, so every screen's lights follow the host's turrets with nothing sent. `stateAt(x, y, z)` (`on`, `off` or `none`) is what the aim's tag reads. A Light that has power is also a lamp: the watcher hands the stage the lens position and aim of each (its optional `LampSink`, in `valley-world.ts` the stage's `atmosphere.lamps`), so the light a Light throws is worked out on every screen from the same list as its lens, with nothing sent.
- `attach-multiplayer.ts` is the one file that knows both the game and the net glue. It hands `multiplayer.ts` the game's player, world, mouse and scene as narrow views and runs it once a frame (`game.onBeforeRender`). Joining a room calls `game.loadWorld(seed)` (unless the valley with that seed is already up with nothing built on it), empties any `?demo=` blocks the new valley raised, and the host's edits are applied to the new world's `BlockWorld`.
- With the run: `attach-multiplayer.ts` also hands the lobby a new `SurvivalSim` whenever the player is on their own (starting at nightfall of night N with `?night=N`) and, unless the page holds a moment (`?time=`, `?shot=`) or runs the demo clock (`?clock`), makes the sky follow the run's clock (`Stage.setSkyDriver`). `create-app.ts` gives the HUD the session's survival view. With `?debug`, `window.__ashbound.mp.clock()` reads the run and `warp(seconds)` jumps it ahead. The sim is given the valley's ground to walk (`valleyNavigation`), so the machines come with it (and the nights' waves, unless `?waves=0`), and `multiplayer.ts` draws them (`enemy-render.ts`) from whichever session is behind the hub, with a flash and a spray of sparks where a breacher goes off (`ShotFx.blast`, on the survival `blast` event); with `?debug`, `mp.spawnCrawlers(n)`, `mp.spawnCrawlerAt(x, z, kind?)` (a plain crawler unless the kind says otherwise: 1 is the armored crawler, 2 the flying skimmer, which hangs in the air anywhere on the map, and 3 the breacher, which walks to a wall or the Core and goes off), `mp.clearEnemies()` and `mp.enemies()` send them in, remove them and list what is on screen, and `mp.hurtPlayer(n, id?)`, `mp.setRespawns(n)` and `mp.warp(seconds)` fell a player, set the crew's charges and play out the wait for the Core to bring them back. A lost run is started over by the HUD's results card; the lobby gives its host the game's way of building the next `SurvivalSim` and a seed for a new valley, and with `?debug`, `mp.restart('retry' | 'new')` does what the card's buttons do.
- `multiplayer.ts` builds the lobby, the room panel, the player's movement over the network and the other players' avatars, and leaves the room when the page goes. `createMultiplayer` takes the narrow views and is updated once a frame; it never touches `Game` directly, so it can be driven from tests. It also draws the run's heaps of debris (`debris-render.ts`) and the scrap thrown off one when it is stripped, and with `?debug`, `mp.heaps()`, `mp.heapsShown()`, `mp.pool()`, `mp.harvest(i)`, `mp.upgrade(x, y, z)`, `mp.setPool()` and `mp.setResearch()` read the economy (the pool, the research points and the tier they open, and each heap with its grade), strip a heap, upgrade a block as E does and set what the crew holds.
- `build-controller.ts` is the player's building hands (and the pulse blaster in slot 5: with it in hand the left button fires along the camera's view instead of breaking, at the blaster's pace, and the host's `sh` report of a hit marks the HUD), with no DOM and no three.js: the hotbar selection, the kind of shape (block, slab or wedge) and how it is turned (the module slot, slot 6, holds a module instead: Q chooses the next one and R turns it through its four facings), Build Mode, what the cursor would do (`src/shared/build-aim.ts`), which heap of debris it is on (`src/systems/economy/aim.ts`, stripped with E at the hands' own pace) and whether it is on a Research Terminal in reach, with no heap in front of it, whose read-only research ladder E then opens (5d2; E, freeing the mouse, going down, walking a block past reach or the Terminal going shut it), and, with neither a heap nor a Terminal, the block of a better material, or the turret of a better level, the cursor's piece could become, which E asks the host for (5e, and 5f2 for turrets; `hudState` carries the piece and, in Build Mode, the reason it would be refused), the cell whose power the screen rings on the ground and how far it reaches (`reach`: the Generator or Power Node in hand where a click would put it, else the one the cursor is on, 5g1 and 5g2) and whether the turret or Node the cursor is on has power (`power` through the `power` port, and `powerModule`, for the tag, 5g2), what the economy would say to a placement (the pool, the research and the heaps through its `economy` port, asked by the same rules as the host's, so a ghost that is not red is a block the host will take), held-button repeat, and the commands. A click becomes a `BlockCommand` for the session and nothing else; the world changes when the host's event comes back, and a refusal comes back as words for the HUD.
- `build-controls.ts` puts those hands on the running game: the mouse buttons, the number keys, the wheel, B, Q and R from `Input`; the aim from the camera each frame; the ghost block (in the shape a click would place) and the break outline (in the shape of the block it marks) on the valley view, and the ring of a module's reach in Build Mode (`world.view.reach`); the session (`setSession`, set when the network code has loaded) and its refusals. The interact key (E) goes to the hands too, and strips the heap of debris the cursor is on, opens a Terminal's ladder or upgrades the block it is on, in Build Mode or out of it. The pool and the heaps the hands ask about are the session's (`session.economy`), and none (nothing charged) until there is one. It gets the blocks ready (`warmUp`) when Build Mode starts, or three seconds after the mouse is first taken, whichever comes first.
- `shot-effects.ts` decides what the pulse blaster's shots look like on this screen: the player's own bolt is drawn at the click from what the screen shows, the host's report adds the burst of a machine that fell, and everyone else's bolts leave the shooter's avatar when the report arrives. A turret's report (it names the turret's cell and no player, 5f1) is drawn on every screen alike: an amber bolt from the muzzle the cell and the end of the bolt give (`turretMuzzle`), with the head of the model standing there turned to the end of the bolt (`aimTurret`, `ModuleRenderer.aim`). A heavier turret's bolt is drawn heavier: the screen reads the turret's level from its own world (`levelOfTurret`) and gives the light the table's `heft` for it. `multiplayer.ts` owns it and the light (`shot-fx.ts`); with `?debug`, `mp.fire()`, `mp.fireAtMachine()` and `mp.shots()` fire, aim at the nearest machine and list what was drawn.
- `vitals-effects.ts` carries the player's Energy and Health into how they move and work: once a frame it reads their meters and hands the scale to the legs (`Game.setSpeedScale`) and to the hands (`BuildController.setHandScale`), holds the legs and idles the hands while the player is down (`Game.setFrozen`, `BuildController.setDown`), and puts everything back to normal when it stops. `create-app.ts` starts it for the session's own meters and gives the same view to the HUD.
- `player-sync.ts` sends the local player to the session each frame, flags teleports, and puts the player back where the host says.
- `seat-spawn.ts` says where each of the four seats starts: in a row beside the spawn point, above anything built there. `standingAt` finds where feet stand at a point of the ground (on the ground itself, or on top of what is built over it if that leaves no room), and `bed-spawn.ts` uses it too.
- `bed-spawn.ts` says where a Bed puts back a player the Core brings back (5h2): `bedSpawn` takes the standing Beds from the modules the world lists, the nearest to where the player fell first, and gives the ground in front of it, else beside it, else behind it (within 1.5 blocks under the Bed's floor or 0.9 over it), else the top of the Bed if there is room, passing a Bed with none for the next, or null when no Bed has room. `attach-multiplayer.ts` asks it first, with where the player fell, and the seat second; a start, a join and a restart give no place and so use the seat.
- `browser-storage.ts` is storage that never throws (private windows, blocked cookies).
- `intro.ts` is the intro film's controller: it starts the film inside the click that asks for the mouse (so the mouse arrives in hand and the input is held deaf while the film plays), skips it on any key or click, ends it at once when the mouse is let go, and hands the same mouse to the player when it ends. The film's sound starts in the same click (`startSound` wakes the audio hub and makes the sound, unless the player has the volume at nothing, the page is told to be silent or the film is held at a second for a picture), is faded out by a skip or a cut and left to ring out into play when the film ends. It is wanted for every plain Play, whatever the system says about motion: a system that asks for less gets the film with the camera held steady (`calm`) and its flashes at a third, not no film. `attach-intro.ts` is the one file that knows both the film and the valley: it tells the film where the crater, the furrow and the Core are and how to hide what the crash made, and `create-app.ts` loads it only when a film is wanted or asked for, so a page with none pays nothing for its code.
- `title.ts` is the title screen's controller: a plain page opens on it (`titleWanted` in `shared/title.ts` says when), the valley drifting under the name of the game and one Play. Play asks for the mouse inside the click and holds the input deaf, fades the picture to black unlocks the game's audio in that same click (a browser lets a page make sound only after one) and has the intro controller start the film (`play()`; on such a page the controller is `manual`, so the film waits for Play); with the film off, or its code not to be had, it glides the camera into the player's view instead. It watches the mouse until the film or the glide has it: a refusal, or a let-go before then, brings the title back to wait for another Play. See [decisions](../../docs/decisions.md#the-title-screen-2026-10-27).
- `attach-sound.ts` is the valley's sound on the running game (see [decisions](../../docs/decisions.md#phase-6c1-the-sound-of-the-valley-2026-10-27)): once a frame it reads the run's clock, the nearest machine and the Core into a `SoundScene` and hands it to `engine/audio/live.ts`, and it plays the sound the host's words have (`cuesForEvent`). The first click or key press on the page unlocks the audio, so the valley is silent until then; the sound is built as soon as there is an audio context, behind the intro film when one is playing, and stays silent while the film does. A film that begins over a sounding valley takes it away in 0.6 seconds. A page told to be silent, or a volume at nothing, makes no sound at all.
- `render-valley-sound.ts` draws a night of the valley's sound to samples with no one listening (`window.__ashbound.audio.renderValley`, with `?debug`): the same plan, voices and output chain on an offline context, with a script for the player and the crew, so the mix can be measured where nothing can be heard. It lives here and not in `engine` because it reads the night's own waves (`planNight`) and the machines' pace from `systems`.

## Keys

Click the page to take the mouse; Esc gives it back.

| Key                   | Does                                                                                                      |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| W A S D, Shift, Space | Walk, sprint, jump                                                                                        |
| V, F5                 | First or third person (F5 will not reload the page while the mouse is in)                                 |
| P                     | Cycle the graphics preset: Low, Balanced, Ultra (saved)                                                   |
| F3                    | Show or hide the frame overlay                                                                            |
| Right click           | Place the selected block (hold: repeat)                                                                   |
| Left click            | Break the block under the cursor (hold: repeat); with the pulse blaster in hand (key 5), fire it          |
| 1 to 8, mouse wheel   | Pick a hotbar slot (slots 1 to 4 are blocks, 5 is the pulse blaster; the tools after it are placeholders) |
| Q                     | Choose block, slab or wedge for every block slot                                                          |
| R                     | Turn the slab or wedge from the way the aim would put it                                                  |
| B                     | Build Mode: ghost block, caption and key list                                                             |
| Esc                   | Free the mouse, and stop a running benchmark                                                              |

## URL switches

| Switch                         | Does                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `bench=1`                      | Run the benchmark at load (see below)                                                                                                              |
| `benchSeconds`, `benchPresets` | Length of the route per preset (default 30), presets to run (`low,ultra`)                                                                          |
| `debug`                        | Expose `window.__ashbound` for tests and scripts; show the frame overlay                                                                           |
| `quality=low\|balanced\|ultra` | Preset for this visit; it is not saved (the pause panel and P save)                                                                                |
| `shot=<name>`                  | Photo mode: hold the camera at a named viewpoint (see `viewpoints.ts`)                                                                             |
| `time=<moment>`                | Fix the time of day (the sky otherwise follows the run's clock); `clock` runs a compressed demo cycle instead                                      |
| `night=<n>`                    | Start the run at nightfall of night n (1 to 99), to try the later nights                                                                           |
| `waves=0`                      | Keep the nights empty: no waves, for pictures and tests that want a quiet night                                                                    |
| `stock=<n>`                    | Open the crew's pool with n of every material (0 to 9999, held to the pool's ceiling), to build without gathering                                  |
| `research=<n>`                 | Open the run with n research points (0 to 9999), already read by a Terminal, to try the better materials                                           |
| `intro=on\|off`                | `on` plays the intro film at the first Play whatever else is going on, `off` never does (otherwise it plays on a plain page, not under automation) |
| `introAt=<seconds>`            | Hold the intro film at this second, with no mouse needed: for pictures and tests                                                                   |
| `title=on\|off`                | `on` shows the title screen on any page, `off` never does (otherwise it shows on a plain page, not under automation)                               |
| `sound=off`                    | Keep the page silent: no audio context is ever made. (The volume is the player's own setting, in the pause card)                                   |
| `seed=<n>`                     | Build another valley                                                                                                                               |
| `demo=outpost\|swatch\|hall`   | Raise a demo structure before the first frame (`demo` alone: the outpost)                                                                          |
| `terrain=heightfield`          | Mesh the terrain as a smoothed heightfield instead of surface nets                                                                                 |
| `scale=<0..1>`                 | Force the internal resolution and switch the adaptive governor off                                                                                 |
| `hud=0`                        | Hide the whole HUD (player HUD, panels, overlay, room card), for clean shots                                                                       |
| `stats=0`                      | Keep the frame overlay off even when `bench` or `debug` would show it                                                                              |
| `freeze=1`                     | Freeze clouds, wind and water, and draw only when something changes                                                                                |
| `join=CODE`, `name=Ada`        | Join a room on load; the name to play under                                                                                                        |
| `broker=host:port`, `ice=...`  | Another PeerJS server; STUN and TURN servers (`none` for direct only)                                                                              |

The viewpoints are `lake`, `valley`, `canopy`, `crash`, `core` and `reactor` (the Outpost Core from the rim and from a few steps away), `trail`, `outcrop`, `noon`, `sunset`, `dusk`, `night` and seven that look at the demo structures: `outpost` and `gate` for `demo=outpost`, `swatch` and `closeup` for `demo=swatch`, `hall`, `hallside` and `eaves` for `demo=hall`. A viewpoint brings its own time of day unless `time=` says otherwise. For example, `/?shot=lake&quality=balanced&hud=0&stats=0&freeze=1` draws the same frame every time.

## Benchmark

`?bench=1` flies the valley once for each preset (Low, Balanced, Ultra) with the adaptive governor off. Each run is the state machine in `src/engine/bench.ts`: two unmeasured warm-up seconds, then a fixed route (30 seconds by default) counted in simulation ticks, so every machine sees the same views. The route lifts over hills, so the camera never dips under the ground. The pass mark is at least 57 FPS on average and a 95th percentile frame time of at most 18.5 ms. A banner shows progress, Esc stops the run, and at the end a card shows one row per preset with a button that copies the report. The report is Markdown with the graphics adapter, browser, canvas, route, valley build times, and for each preset the internal size, average FPS, 95th percentile, slowest 1 percent, CPU time, GPU time (where the browser offers timer queries), draw calls and triangles. A window larger than 1080p still reports honestly: the internal size column says what was drawn.

A display that refreshes faster than 60 Hz caps the average at its refresh rate, so on a fast machine every preset can read the same (the report states the display rate); the GPU time column is the only one that tells them apart, and it is noisy at the cap. On a laptop with two GPUs, check the report's graphics adapter line: the numbers mean something only if the browser ran on the dedicated GPU (Windows Settings, System, Display, Graphics, High performance for the browser).

Frame cost cannot be measured in the cloud sandbox (software rendering only), so the numbers in the docs for the sandbox are static budgets and relative software costs; the real ones come from this report on real hardware. The first real report, from the look spike, is recorded in [art direction](../../docs/art-direction.md#first-real-measurement).
