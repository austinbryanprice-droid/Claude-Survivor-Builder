# systems

Survival, waves, economy and upgrades: the host simulation. No three.js, DOM, engine or net imports, so it runs in a Worker and under Vitest. Phases 4 and 5 fill this in.

- `survival/sim.ts`: `SurvivalSim`, the run as the host plays it. It advances in fixed 20 Hz steps (`SIM_HZ`), fed real time by `advance(dt)` (a gap past `maxCatchUpSeconds` is dropped, not replayed) or jumped by `warp(seconds)` for tests and the debug hook, and it announces each change of light as an event. It is the day and night clock and the Outpost Core's health: the Core mends by day, is wounded by `hurtCore` (the debug hook until enemies exist), and when it falls the run is over, the clock stops and an `overrun` event goes out. It also keeps a pair of meters for every player in the room (`addPlayer`, `removePlayer`, `observe` where they are, `hurtPlayer`, `spendEnergy` for what they build and fire, `readVitals`) and steps them with the clock. Where the world gives it ground and a Core to walk to (`nav`), it also plays the machines (`enemies`): their bites are wounds to the players and the Core, a block that changes is told to the map (`blockChanged`), and the walls they chew through come out of `drainBreaks` for the host to break. `fire` plays a shot of the pulse blaster: it takes the player's bank of shots, traces the bolt (`src/shared/shot.ts`) against the machines as the shooter saw them, hurts the one it ends on and charges the Energy. The nights' waves are sent in by a `WaveDirector` (`waves`) that it calls once a step; `waves: false` keeps the nights empty. It keeps the crew's respawn charges too: a player at no Health is laid down (`down`), the Core brings the first to fall back after six seconds for one charge (`back`), the dawn adds charges, and when every player is down with the pool empty the run is lost (`overrun` with the cause `crew`). `readVitals` adds the wait to a player's reading. It counts what the run has come to for the results card (`felled`, `placed`, `revived`, `gathered`) until the run is over. Where it has a Core and ground it also keeps the crew's economy (`economy`, below): a dawn uncovers the next heaps of debris, and `gathered` is the materials the crew has stripped from them.
- `survival/vitals.ts`: `PlayerVitals`, one player's Health and Energy as the host keeps them, with no clock of its own: Energy drains by living, sprinting, wounds and building, is refilled in the Core's charge field and slows the legs and the hands below a quarter of the bar (`speedScaleFor`, `handScaleFor`); Health mends after a quiet spell while there is Energy. It also keeps the bank of the pulse blaster (`takeShot`: two shots, refilled at one every `BLASTER.interval`, slower with low Energy). A player at no Health is down: nothing about them changes until `revive` stands them up with full Health, half the Energy and a spell of being shaken (Energy drains faster, the blaster hits softer: `damageScale`). What leaves it is whole points.
- `survival/nav.ts`: `NavGrid`, the map the machines walk by: the ground round the Core in one-block columns (open, blocked, walled by something the players built, or the Core) and the cost of the cheapest walk from each to the Core, with a wall priced at the time it takes to chew. It is worked out again, at most four times a second, after a block changes, and an enemy asks it which way to go (`aim`), whether a straight walk is clear (`lineClear`) and where it may stand (`pointClear`, `canEnter`).
- `survival/enemies.ts`: `EnemySim`, the machines as the host plays them: spawning on a ring round the Core from the run's seed, then each step one of bite, chase, chew or march, crowding apart, and the walls they chew through. Wounds go out through a `Strikes` interface the survival simulation answers, so it has no idea what a player is. `spawn` takes a `SpawnPlan` (its own random stream and the stretch of the ring a wave comes from). `write` fills the wire's `EnemyState` list.
- `survival/waves.ts`: the waves of each night and the director that sends them in. `planNight` (with `machinesOnNight`, `kindsOnNight` and `healthScaleOnNight`) is arithmetic on the night's number: how many machines, of which kinds, how tough, in how many waves, and when each is announced and comes. `WaveDirector` plays that plan into the machines one step at a time (a machine every 0.4 s, each wave from one side of the ring drawn from the world's seed and the night and wave numbers), announces each wave ten seconds ahead and clears the field at dawn. It keeps no clock of its own.
- `survival/valley-nav.ts`: `valleyNavigation`, the one place that joins a valley (its terrain and its blocks) to the map's `NavWorld`, so the app and the tests build it the same way.
- `survival/balance.ts`: the table of numbers that shape the loop (the Core's health and how fast it mends by day, `VITALS`: both meters, what spends and refills Energy, and what low Energy does, `ENEMIES`: the field's size and where it spawns, and the crawler's body, speed, bite, reach, hitbox and appetite for walls, `BLASTER`: damage, pace, range and the bank of the pulse blaster, `WAVES`: how many machines come each night, of which kinds, how tough, in how many waves and when, and `RESPAWN`: the charges each player brings and each dawn adds, how long a player lies down, and what coming back leaves them with). The length of the day and the night is `DAY_SECONDS` and `NIGHT_SECONDS` in `src/shared/daycycle.ts`, because the sky reads them too.
- `economy/economy.ts`: `Economy`, one run's shared pool and field of debris as the host keeps them. `harvest(index, by)` strips a heap (the yield of a strip is a hash of the seed, the heap and the strip's number, `stripYield`, so a run plays the same every time), takes what fits into the pool and says what happened (a `HarvestEvent`, or why not: nothing there, nothing left, the pool full); `nodeAt` finds the heap a cell names, `dawn(night)` uncovers the heaps of that night, and `read`, `leftOf`, `gathered` and `version` are what the screens read. It keeps no clock; the survival simulation and the session tell it what happens.
- `economy/pool.ts`: `Pool`, the crew's materials: a whole count of each (in tier order) under one ceiling, with `add` (as much as fits), `take` (all or nothing), `has`, `room` and `set`.
- `economy/field.ts`: `debrisField`, the run's whole field of heaps as a pure function of the seed, the ground the machines walk and the Core's place: the first batch in a band round the Core, each later batch (uncovered at a dawn) in a band a little farther out, on dry, level ground clear of each other and of the rim, written to a hundredth of a block. `debrisBand` is where a batch lies.
- `economy/aim.ts`: `aimAtDebris`, which heap the cursor is on: the nearest ball (the heap's size round a point a little above its foot) that the view's ray enters within the player's reach and in front of the first block or hill, as pure arithmetic a test can drive.
- `economy/balance.ts`: the numbers of the economy in two tables: `POOL` (the opening stock and the ceiling) and `DEBRIS` (how many heaps, where they lie, how many strips each gives and what a strip yields, how big a heap is, how far a player reaches, how close the cursor must be and how fast a player can strip).
