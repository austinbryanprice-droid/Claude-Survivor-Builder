# net

Multiplayer: the host's authority, the guests, and the transports that carry them. One command path serves single-player and rooms alike. This layer imports no three.js and touches no DOM, and it reaches PeerJS only through the small interface in `peer-transport.ts`. [docs/multiplayer.md](../../docs/multiplayer.md) explains the design; the message shapes are in `src/shared/protocol.ts` and `wire.ts`.

- `session.ts`: the `GameSession` interface the game talks to (`sendCommand`, `publishState`, `sampleRemotes`, events, roster, phase) and `WorldBinding`, how a session reaches the game's world.
- `host-session.ts`: the authority. Plays single-player with no room, opens a room, validates every command, fans events out, checks guests' movement, relays everyone's state, remembers departed guests by token. It also runs the survival simulation (`src/systems/survival`) from its own timer and tells the guests the time and the crew's respawn charges (`sv`), each change of light and each player down or back (`se`) and each guest its own Health, Energy and countdowns (`vt`); every player in the room has a pair of meters in the simulation, which pays for the blocks they place and break and refuses them to a player who is down, and alone, the run waits for the player (`setLocalActive`). When the Core brings a player back it moves them to their seat (`placeAtBase`, with the same `fix` as a failed movement check, or a correction on its own screen for the host's player). It also sends the machines' list on the fast lane ten times a second (and once a second while the field is empty, which the lane could otherwise lose), and breaks the walls the machines chew through, as the world's own change. It decides the players' shots too (`fire`, from a guest or from its own player, answered to everyone with `sh`), judging each against the machines as the shooter's screen drew them. A lost run is started over by `restartRun`: the world is rebuilt for the seed, a new simulation is asked of the game, every player is seated again and each guest is sent a `run` frame (4h).
- `guest-session.ts`: dials a room, handshakes, builds the host's world from the snapshot, requests edits, applies events, keeps the clock in step, and starts over when the host does (`run`: forget the old run, rebuild the world for the host's seed, stand at the seat).
- `transport.ts`: what the sessions need from a network (`Transport`, `HostEndpoint`, `Link`) and `NetError`.
- `loopback.ts`: that network in memory, with latency, jitter, loss, reordering and silent deaths, for tests and the single-player host.
- `peer-transport.ts`: the real one, over PeerJS and WebRTC. Two lanes per guest, room codes with a prefix and collision retry, the broker dropping out and coming back, configurable broker and ICE.
- `lobby.ts`: alone, hosting or visiting, and the buttons between them (host, join, cancel, leave, play alone), plus why a room ended. The room panel draws its `state`.
- `hub.ts`: `SessionHub`, the one `GameSession` the game holds whichever session is behind it. It forwards the survival view and the player's presence the same way.
- `survival-mirror.ts`: a guest's copy of the survival clock: the host's last `sv` word (the clock, the Core's health, the crew's respawn charges, what the run has come to and whether it is over and why) carried forward on the guest's own timer, never ticking backwards, and the events the host announces. The host reads its simulation directly (`HostSession.survival`), and the screen cannot tell which it has.
- `vitals-mirror.ts`: a guest's copy of its own meters: the host's last `vt` word (whole points of Health and Energy, whether it stands in the Core's field, and the seconds until the Core brings it back and the seconds it stays shaken), from which the slow-down that low Energy brings is worked out by the same rule as the host's, so every screen agrees and no speed travels. The host reads its player's meters from the simulation (`HostSession.vitals`).
- `enemy-mirror.ts`: `EnemyMirror`, the machines as a screen sees them: the last eight reports of each, sampled a little in the past (150 ms behind for a guest, 70 for the host) so a crowd moves smoothly, with a short carry-on when a report is late and a jump rather than a glide for a long hop. The host feeds it from its simulation and a guest from the lists the host sends on the fast lane; the screen cannot tell which.
- `scheduler.ts`, `emitter.ts`, `random.ts`: timers on real or virtual time, listener sets, and crypto-backed random numbers.
